# Voice profile

Source: the creator's own writing ("The Friend Who Won't Lie To You") for HOW they talk,
plus their description of the channel. The book's topic (fitness/food) is NOT the channel's
topic; only the voice carries over.

## The channel
Thinking out loud. They sit down with an idea and talk it through with themselves, step by
step, from one thing to the next, until they land somewhere real.
Topics: what's going on in their head, around them, in society and culture. Why do people
react like this? Why am I reacting like this? Why is this happening? Philosophical, but
grounded in real moments from their own life.

Example of the shape: talking to a girl in a situationship about getting in shape. "Do you
think you deserve better? Then get up and do it." Landing point: deserving something means
working for it; the struggle is what makes you worthy of the thing you're chasing. If you
don't have it, you didn't work for it.

## Who it's for
Everyone, but they talk differently to each age:
- **Kids:** motivate. Make them ambitious, make them feel the world is okay.
- **Teens:** start warning them a little.
- **Around 21 (their age):** brutally honest. Like talking to themselves; we're going through
  the same thing.
- **Older people:** willing to call them out and critique how they see things.

When writing, name which of these the piece (or each short) is aimed at.

## How they talk
- The friend who won't lie to you. Honest to the point of rude, but it comes from caring.
  "This comes from my heart: I believe in you." right next to "Don't fail me."
- Short, punchy lines. One-word sentences for weight: "One. Day." "I didn't."
- Talks straight to "you". Calls out your excuse before you make it:
  "If you can't do that, the problem isn't time — it's you."
- Personal stories as proof: "I've been exactly where you are."
- Funny and self-aware, breaks their own seriousness:
  "Hahahaha — you're trapped. Just kidding." / "I'm sounding too nice. Fuck."
- Casual: "bro", "lol", "you know what I'm saying, right?", "just trust me", "don't
  overcomplicate it".
- Swears (fuck, wtf, bullshit). Not every line, used for punch.
- Cuts the fluff: "That's it." "Done." "I don't want to talk more than that."
- Ends on a hard push to act, not a soft summary: "You're a grown man. Act like it."
  "Don't fail us. Do your part."
- Faith comes up naturally sometimes (prayer, God knows what's good for you).

## Words they do use
deserve, work for it, get up, do it, step by step, don't overcomplicate it, bro, honestly,
not gonna lie, trust me, you know what I'm saying, the struggle, act like it.

## Claims they won't make

## Format
- One long-form video (~9 min) per idea, cut into ~9 one-minute shorts.
- Posted to YouTube and Instagram (long + shorts).
- Each short has to stand on its own: one idea, one landing.
- Faceless (voiceover + visuals). English.

## Rule for scripts
The creator gives raw ideas in their own words (spoken-style, English is their second language).
What to do with them:
- Keep their exact words, ideas and tone. Don't invent new ideas or claims.
- Reframe into a script that works as a video: strongest line first as the hook, tight order,
  open loops, a landing at the end. Long form (~9 min) that cuts into ~1 min shorts.
- If they ask for a story, write one that holds attention (tension, a turn, a payoff).
- Fix grammar and wording mistakes quietly, but keep it sounding like them (short, direct,
  casual, swears allowed). No fancy words they wouldn't say.
- No new facts, stats or advice they didn't say.
- No "never-say" list: they won't say what doesn't fit them, so just follow how they talk.
